## What this portfolio is designed to prove

Each promoted artifact should answer:

- What was built?
- What did Michael personally contribute?
- Why does it matter?
- What evidence can a recruiter or technical reviewer inspect?
- What limitations and maturity boundaries remain?

Important work is evaluated in three layers:

1. **Engineering artifact** — code, configuration, architecture, automation, tests, deployment, and repository.
2. **Evidence artifact** — test results, logs, evaluations, before/after results, screenshots, and independent review.
3. **Presentation artifact** — recruiter case study, technical walkthrough, client-style sample, diagram, demo, or executive summary.

## Evidence and disclosure rules

- Project-specific repositories remain authoritative for technical implementation and raw evidence.
- Original drift evidence is immutable. Sanitized or standardized portfolio materials must be derived copies with provenance to the untouched original.
- Independent review claims must identify the reviewer, date, scope, methodology, human versus AI involvement, limitations, and what was actually validated.
- Prototypes, homelab deployments, independent projects, production systems, client work, experiments, and AI-assisted work must be labeled accurately.
- Public artifacts must exclude credentials, tokens, private correspondence, personal/legal/health information, sensitive infrastructure details, and other private identifiers.
- Microsoft affiliation or partner wording must not be published until the exact status is verified from authoritative evidence. Do not claim a Solutions Partner designation or badge without proof of that exact designation.
